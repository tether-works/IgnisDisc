Burn CDs and DVDs, rip music CDs, and back up folders on Windows.

IgnisDisc is a free, open-source disc burner for Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit). No extra drivers, no ads, no bundled offers, and no tracking.

Download Installer (IgnisDisc-Setup.exe) · v1.16 · 83.5 MB Portable .zip · v1.16 · 82.9 MB

First run: installs for your user account, no administrator rights needed. The download is not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may warn the first time: click More info → Run anyway, or check the SHA-256 checksums below.

  • Data discs Burn files, folders and .iso images to CD or DVD. Nero, Alcohol 120%, CloneCD and DiscJuggler images work too.
  • DVD-Video Turn movie files into discs that play in a DVD player, with a title menu, chapters and subtitles (from .srt files or optional speech recognition). Long movies are compressed to fit a 4.7 GB DVD. A dual-layer DVD (8.5 GB) has room for nearly twice the video bitrate, so the picture stays sharper: the screen offers one whenever it would help, and asks for one when the movies run longer than about 4 h 26 min.
  • Audio CDs From WAV, FLAC, MP3, Opus, Ogg, M4A or WMA tracks, with optional loudness levelling and CD-Text.
  • Rip music CDs To WAV, FLAC, MP3, Opus or AAC, with track names and cover art filled in for you.
  • Multi-disc backups Split a large folder across numbered discs, with PAR2 recovery data to rebuild damaged files.
  • Checked every time After a burn it reads the disc back, compares it with what you asked for, and tells you when it is done.

What works, and how it was tested

DVD-Video authoring[ PARTIAL ][ PASS ]Requires ffmpeg.exe (the 1-click download). Long movies are compressed to fit: before converting, the DVD-Video screen reads each movie's running time and says whether they fit a 4.7 GB DVD at full quality, compressed (with the video bitrate and how it will look), only on a dual-layer DVD, or on no DVD at all (Burn is then off). When a 4.7 GB DVD would have to compress and a dual-layer DVD would keep a clearly higher bitrate, it offers both (for example 3,587 kbps or full quality for a 2 h 20 min film), and a burn is refused before converting when the disc in the drive is too small. Each film becomes a title with a chapter every 5 minutes, English subtitles from an .srt beside it (or generated with the optional subtitle tool; the DVD-Video screen shows each movie's subtitle source with Choose .srt... and No subtitles), and a title menu with a still from each film and Play / Play with subtitles buttons. The navigation tables follow libdvdread's structures and were checked with libdvdnav (mpv, VLC). Played on one stand-alone player so far, an Xbox Series X: a film with menu, chapters and subtitles on a DVD+RW, and two films on a dual-layer DVD+R played across the layer change. Discs authored before 2026-11-14 did not play there
Generate subtitles for DVD-Video (speech recognition)[ PARTIAL ][ N/A ]Optional 1-click download, nothing bundled: whisper.cpp 1.9.5 and its models, every file pinned by SHA-256 (install-subtitle-tool, or the button on the DVD-Video screen). On a PC with an NVIDIA GPU and driver 551.61 or newer it gets the CUDA build and the large-v3-turbo model (2.3 GB download); every other PC gets the processor build and the smaller base model (160 MB). AMD and Intel GPUs are not used: whisper.cpp publishes no Windows GPU build for them. Measured on one PC, on an 86-minute animated feature with English dialogue, against its human-made subtitles: on an RTX 3090, 3.3 min from movie file to .srt (26 times real time) with a word error rate of 24.9%; processor build only (16-core desktop processor, base model), 3.4 min and 34.3%. The large model on that processor ran at 1.7 times real time (178 s for 5 minutes, about 50 minutes for this film), so the better model needs a GPU. The error rate counts wording the human subtitles shorten too; an invented place name was never heard right. The subtitles are machine-generated: check them before relying on them. burn-dvd --generate-subtitles ("Generate subtitles" on the DVD-Video screen) makes them for movies with no .srt beside them and puts them on the disc as its subtitle track. Authored end to end on the test PC from a 5-minute excerpt: the generated lines appear as the DVD subtitle track and on the menu's "Play with subtitles" button; such a disc has not yet been burned and played in a stand-alone player. Muxing the subtitle track needs a recent FFmpeg (the 1-click download): a 2021 build crashed on it
Blu-ray (BD-R / BD-RE)[ N/A ][ N/A ]Not yet: Blu-ray discs are recognised (type, size, layers) and archive sets can be planned for them, and the experimental direct writer handles single-layer BD-R and BD-RE in the drive simulator, but burning one has never been tried on a real drive
Burn with direct drive commands (experimental)[ PARTIAL ][ PASS ]Audio CDs Session-at-Once and data/video discs without the Windows writer (--native on the command line, or "Direct drive commands" in the app — on by default in the app for audio CDs, falling back to the Windows writer if the drive does not offer Session-at-Once, for dual-layer DVD-Video, and for a DVD-RW, in the app and with burn-data and burn-iso, where a quick-erased disc that cannot be left open still goes to the Windows writer (a Settings switch, or --windows-writer on the command line, turns the DVD-RW default off); off by default elsewhere); the disc is always read back and compared. Run on real hardware on one drive so far (LG GP60NB50), on CD-R and CD-RW. Mixed-mode CDs (a Mode 1 data track, then audio) from a .cue file too: one CD-RW so far, where Windows read the data track's files back identical. Blank single-layer DVD-R, DVD-RW and DVD+R can also be written this way. A further session on a CD-R, CD-RW, DVD-R, DVD-RW or DVD+R left open is added the same way: on real discs on the same drive, three sessions each on a CD-RW, a CD-R and a DVD+R and four on a DVD-R, every file of every session identical (the first DVD-R try was refused with nothing written, because the drive rejects the writer's old track query on DVD; fixed before these runs); on a DVD-RW too, which must be fully erased before it can be left open: a second session added and the disc closed, every file of both sessions identical (Windows showed the new session after the disc was put back in). On real hardware so far two DVD-Rs on the same drive (Verbatim): one closed in one pass (372 MB), one left open, after which Windows' own writer added a second session; every file of each session identical when read through Windows. Two DVD+RWs too (Verbatim, 372 MB each, every file identical): a used one written over with leave to replace its data, and a new one, formatted first by the drive. A single-layer DVD+R too (Verbatim, 198 MB, finalized by the drive, every file identical). A DVD-RW too (Ritek): closed in one pass after a quick erase (63 MB; the drive took 6 minutes to close it), left open after a full erase (200 MB), and in overwrite mode written over past its formatted part, which the writer expanded first (230 MB); every file identical each time. A DVD-Video disc was written to a used DVD+RW this way too (a 60 s test card, 44 MB): every sector read back and the title decodes in full, this test card was not played in a stand-alone player (a later film disc was; see DVD-Video authoring). A dual-layer DVD+R too (Verbatim, the Ubuntu 26.04 image, 6.5 GB, with the layer break moved to where it was asked for; the drive reported it there and the disc's SHA-256 equalled the image). A two-film DVD-9 (103 and 86 minutes) went onto a dual-layer DVD+R the same way, with the layer break on the cell where the second film starts: every sector read back, the drive reported the break there, and an Xbox Series X played through the first film into the second across the layer change without a stop. Dual-layer DVD-R (layer jump recording, on drives that offer it), DVD-RAM, single-layer BD-R and BD-RE, leaving an audio CD open for a second data session (CD-Extra), and the optional Audio CD PAR2 recovery session (--recovery-session / rip-audio --repair) have run only in the built-in drive simulator, not on a real disc. Dual-layer DVD-R on a drive without layer jump recording, and multi-layer Blu-ray discs, still use the Windows writer
CD-Text / ISRC on burned audio CDs[ PARTIAL ][ PASS ]The Windows writer cannot write lead-in CD-Text. The direct writer (on by default on the Audio CD screen when the drive offers Session-at-Once, or --native on burn-audio) writes CD-Text, ISRC and catalog codes, then reads the disc back and compares it. Run on real hardware on one drive so far (LG GP60NB50): a CD-RW and a CD-R, where CD-Text, ISRC and catalog codes and the audio all read back correctly; not yet checked in a stand-alone CD player
DVD+R book type (bitsetting)[ N/A ][ PASS ]Needs drive-vendor-specific commands; reports "not supported" instead of pretending
Test write (simulation)[ PARTIAL ][ PASS ]With the experimental direct writer (--native --test-write): the whole burn with the laser off. Run on one drive (LG GP60NB50) on CD-R and CD-RW. DVD-R has passed a test write on the same drive (a Verbatim DVD-R, still blank after a reload); DVD-RW only in the drive simulator so far; DVD+R discs have none. The Windows writer has no test write

Technical overview

Built with.NET 10 WPF desktop app (IgnisDisc.exe) and a command-line tool (ignisdisc-cli.exe)
Disc imagesOwn builder for ISO 9660 + Joliet + UDF bridge volumes (UDF 1.02 by default, 1.50 to 2.60 on request), El Torito BIOS/UEFI boot images, optional SHA256SUMS / BLAKE3SUMS lists. Reads and converts .nrg, .mdf/.mds, .ccd/.img and .cdi
DVD-VideoIFO and NAV pack tables, still-frame title menu, 5-minute chapters, English subtitles from .srt or the optional whisper.cpp download, layer break on a cell boundary for dual-layer DVD-9
Audio CDsBS.1770-4 -18 LUFS gated loudness normalisation; gapless lossy decoding through the optional FFmpeg download
WritingWindows IMAPIv2 by default for data discs and single-layer DVDs. In the app, audio CDs (drives that report Session-at-Once) and dual-layer DVD-Video use IgnisDisc's own SCSI MMC commands by default; --native on the command line
Reading & checksWin32 SCSI pass-through: 294-byte C2 error pointers, AccurateRip checksums and read-offset detection. After a burn, a sector-by-sector comparison read from the disc, not the drive's buffer
ArchivesPer-disc SHA-256 manifests; PAR2 recovery split over inner, middle and outer zones, each with a copy of the volume descriptor
TestingIn-memory virtual drives stand in for hardware in the test suite

CdBurner.Core

  • IsoUdfImageBuilder: ISO 9660, Joliet UCS-2BE, OSTA UDF 1.02 to 2.60 (metadata partition and mirror from 2.50), El Torito BIOS/UEFI boot catalogs; byte-identical output with a fixed build time.
  • DvdVideoAuthoringService, DvdIfoGenerator, DvdNavPackWriter, and DvdStillMenu: VMG/VTS navigation tables, still-frame title menus (Play / Play with subtitles), 5-minute chapters, DvdSubtitleWriter (.srt or SubtitleGenerationService via optional whisper.cpp 1.9.5), and DvdCellLayerBreak for dual-layer DVD-9 discs.
  • Streaming LegacyDiscImageConverter for .nrg, .mdf/.mds, and .ccd/.img (incl. Mode 2 / XA 2336B/2448B sectors), and DiscJuggler .cdi (v2, v3, v3.5; tested on hand-built files only).
  • RedBookPcmConverter (polyphase resampler, TPDF dither, BS.1770-4 -18 LUFS gated loudness with -1 dBTP 4x-oversampled limiter, gapless FFmpeg decoding for .opus/.ogg/.mp3), built-in FlacFileCodec, CD picture tracks (.cue pipeline, simulator-tested), and ArchiveSpanPlanner with 3-zone PAR2 recovery and SHA-256 / BLAKE3 manifests.

CdBurner.Hardware

  • Background ApartmentState.MTA worker (MtaBurnWorker) driving IDiscFormat2Data, IDiscFormat2TrackAtOnce, IDiscFormat2RawCD, and IDiscFormat2Erase.
  • PhysicalSectorReader + Win32ScsiTransport (IOCTL_SCSI_PASS_THROUGH_DIRECT, Full TOC 0x02, READ CD with C2 pointers, READ (10) with FUA for the check after a burn).
  • In-memory virtual drives for tests (VirtualDiscBackend, VirtualMmcDrive) and a compatibility test matrix of 32 simulated drive and media combinations.

CdBurner.Cli & App

  • IgnisDisc.exe: WPF desktop studio with 8 dedicated workspaces, OS-synced Dark/Light theme (ThemeManager), per-movie DVD subtitle controls, burn-finished notice (sound + taskbar flash), instant-save settings, eject-after-burn on by default, and pinned-SHA-256 1-click downloads for optional ffmpeg, par2cmdline-turbo 1.5.0, and whisper.cpp 1.9.5.
  • ignisdisc-cli.exe: 37 commands with --json output; it refuses to replace the contents of a rewritable disc unless --overwrite is given.
  • Per-user installer (net48 bootstrapper, no administrator rights) with a small separate Uninstall-IgnisDisc.exe.

Direct drive commands (experimental)

  • Audio CDs: Session-at-Once with SEND CUE SHEET and WRITE (10): CD-Text in the lead-in (R-W sub-channel), ISRC and catalog codes, pre-emphasis and copy bits, INDEX 00 gaps, and audio in front of track 1.
  • Data discs: CD Track-at-Once; DVD-R and DVD-RW Disc-At-Once, or in packets when left open; DVD+R written in sequence and finalized; DVD+RW written over from sector 0. A disc left open gets its next session the same way, with the earlier files kept. Speed is taken from the drive's own list.
  • Checks: every direct burn is read back and compared (track list, CD-Text, codes, every audio sample or data sector). A laser-off test write is available, and every drive command is logged.
  • When it is used: on by default in the app for audio CDs (when the drive offers Session-at-Once) and dual-layer DVD-Video, and for a DVD-RW in the app and with burn-data / burn-iso (Windows' writer formats a DVD-RW for overwriting; a Settings switch or --windows-writer turns this off). Elsewhere it is off by default (--native on the command line) and IMAPIv2 writes, which is also the fallback.
  • Tested on real discs (one drive, LG GP60NB50): audio and data CDs on CD-R and CD-RW; a mixed-mode CD (data track, then audio) on a CD-RW; data on DVD-R, DVD+R and DVD+RW; a DVD-RW closed in one pass, left open and given a second session, and in overwrite mode expanded and written over; a 60 s DVD-Video test disc on a DVD+RW, read back in full; a 6.5 GB dual-layer DVD+R with the layer break where it was asked for (SHA-256 equal to the image); a two-film DVD-9 played on an Xbox Series X across the layer change; three or four sessions added in turn on a CD-R, a CD-RW, a DVD-R and a DVD+R, every file read back identical.
  • Simulator only so far: DVD-RAM, dual-layer DVD-R, Blu-ray, CD-Extra / Audio CD PAR2 recovery sessions, and CD picture tracks.
